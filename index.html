Schumann Resonance

Earth’s electromagnetic cavity · ~7.83 Hz fundamental

Data card via MeteoAgent. Values shift with ionospheric conditions; “7.83 Hz” is the long-term average of the fundamental mode, not a fixed constant.

What it is

The Schumann resonances are a set of peaks in the extremely low frequency (ELF) electromagnetic field spectrum of the Earth–ionosphere cavity. Global lightning continuously excites standing waves in the cavity between the surface and the lower ionosphere (~50–100 km).

The fundamental mode is observed near 7.83 Hz. Higher modes appear near 14.3, 20.8, 27.3, and 33.8 Hz. These frequencies move slightly with day/night changes in ionospheric height, geomagnetic activity, and lightning distribution. The signal strength is extremely small (picotesla range).

Named after physicist Winfried Otto Schumann, who predicted the resonances in 1952. First measured in the 1960s.

Ideal cavity calculation gives higher values (~10.6 Hz fundamental). Real ionosphere is lossy, lowering the observed peaks.
